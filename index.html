Take it with you

Your object

Put something on a turntable and film it.

Use a plain backdrop, a lamp that does not move, and one slow full turn. Drop the video here, or six or more photos taken at even steps around the object. Nothing is uploaded; the files never leave this tab.

  • Camera fixed, object centred, backdrop one colour.
  • Photos are read in filename order and assumed evenly spaced over 360 degrees.
  • Shiny, see-through or backdrop-coloured objects will not carve well.

How it carves

Every photo is a cookie cutter.

  1. Cut out the object. Each photo is compared with its own backdrop. What differs is the silhouette.
  2. Start with a block. A cube of voxels is placed where the object stands.
  3. Cut with each view. A voxel that lands outside a silhouette cannot be part of the object, so it goes. Every photo removes one more wedge.
  4. Paint what is left. Surface voxels take their colour from the photos that can see them.

Limits, measured

What a silhouette cannot see.

A hull is the biggest shape that fits inside every silhouette. It can never be smaller than the object, and it is bigger wherever the object is hollow or dented. The mug is the clearest case: its inside is invisible from outside, so the hull fills it and the overlap figure stays around 40%.

Two heights of camera tighten the hull a lot. The demo alternates between a low and a high ring. With one ring only, the stool scores 59% overlap with its true shape instead of 83%, because the space under the seat stays ambiguous.

Real photos are harder than the demo. Shadows get read as object, and a backdrop-coloured object gets cut out. Mask accuracy on the demo is measured against exact silhouettes, so treat it as an upper bound.
